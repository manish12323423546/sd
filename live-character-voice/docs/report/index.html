00:02 · Why "Synced" removes the delay

One line, on a stopwatch

A line can only be re-spoken after the commentator has finished it, because the words have to be heard first. Synced mode holds the picture and the original sound back a few seconds, like a TV broadcast delay, so the new voice can start exactly when the original line starts. Move the sliders. The stage times are the measured medians.

Keeping one label per person

Up to 8 speakers now. Each person gets a bank of voice-prints; a new person is only created from a clear, long, non-shouted line; two labels that turn out to be one person are merged and earlier lines relabelled; known commentators can be enrolled from Voice Lab samples. Why it is hard: two lines of the same person score 0.37–0.48 alike typically but as low as 0.02–0.12 when one is shouted; two different people score 0.05 typically but up to 0.66. Bigger voice-print models (WeSpeaker ResNet34 / ResNet293) and noise reduction were tested and measured worse. What helps most: enrol the commentators (10–20 s each). Remaining errors: similar voices, and 3–4 % of speech in phrases that hold two people during very fast hand-overs. Video (who is on screen) helps only for studio panels where speakers are visible; it needs a GPU and is on the roadmap. Full scenario table: docs/SPEAKERS.md.

00:04 · Your questions

Straight answers

Is the crowd-noise part solved? Can I mute the commentators and keep only the crowd?

Partly, and only for stereo streams. The extension's side panel now has Original sound: Full / Crowd only / Off. "Crowd only" removes whatever is mixed in the centre of a stereo picture. Commentary usually is; the crowd usually is spread wide.

  • Measured on a stereo test mix: commentary exactly centred → removed completely; 1 dB off-centre → 21 dB quieter (about a tenth as loud); 2 dB → 15 dB quieter. The crowd stays.
  • Mono streams cannot be split this way. The panel detects mono and says so.
  • Clean separation on any stream needs an AI separation model (Demucs/MDX-style, or a paid API such as AudioShake). That needs a GPU server or a paid API. It is on the roadmap below.
Why did live streams feel slow, with voices on top of each other and lines missing?

Two causes were found and both are fixed in this build:

  • Free transcription limit. Groq's free tier allows about 20 requests a minute; a busy commentary needs about 20. When refused, lines fell back to a slower local model (up to 5.4 s per line, slower still on the 2-CPU hosted server). The engine now paces itself under the limit and records which engine handled each line ("Groq 54 · local 6" in the latest test). The full fix is Groq's paid tier at $0.04 per hour of audio, which raises the limit about 20×.
  • Overlapping voices. A line still being generated was scheduled with an estimated length. When the real voice ran longer, the next one started on top of it. The relay now plays one voice at a time and waits for the real end.

In Live overlay mode a voice always comes about a line late (≈ 5 s), because a line has to be heard before it can be re-spoken. Use the Synced window for on-time voices.

Is the analysis accurate (transcript, speakers, emotion)?
  • Transcript: 93.5 % of words right (Groq Whisper large-v3 turbo). Most errors are player names ("Moreau" → "Morrow") and very short shouts ("Save!" → "Dave").
  • What the voices say: 92.2 % right, 7 of 525 words missing in the latest test.
  • Speakers: 94.3 % (2 commentators), 96.0 % (3 commentators).
  • Emotion: 55 % exactly right, 94 % within one level. Calm lines are usually read as normal. This is the weakest part.

These numbers come from synthetic test matches whose script is known. Real broadcasts with crowd noise, accents and overlapping speech will score lower until tested. The Quality Lab can score any clip that has a script, so a few real minutes with a typed transcript would give real-world numbers.

Hugging Face or Vercel: which is better to host on and send to a client?

Hugging Face, for the engine. Vercel can only host the web pages. Vercel Functions cap code at 500 MB (the engine's AI parts are over 1 GB), cut connections after 300 s on your plan, and do not keep one session on one server. The Space runs the whole app on one link, keeps live connections open, and updates itself from GitHub. Vercel adds a faster front page but no new function. Send the Hugging Face link.

If I send the link to someone, what do they get? Do they see my cloned voices?
  • They get everything you see: Studio, Live capture, Voice Lab, Quality Lab, reports, after entering the access code.
  • Yes, they see and can use your cloned voices, and they can delete them or add their own. There are no separate accounts yet; everyone shares one workspace.
  • Only one session runs at a time on the free server. A second person gets "busy".
  • Usage spends your Groq and Fish credit.
  • Cloned voices are lost when the Space is rebuilt from scratch (normal updates keep them).
  • For the extension, send them the extension folder (zip). They load it in Chrome with Developer mode and enter the link + access code in the popup's Engine field.
Does it need post-training (training our own models)?

Not to work, and not for the next big gains. Everything uses ready models plus our logic. Training helps later, only with data you collect:

  • Emotion: the best candidate. Label 1–2 hours of real commentary lines (calm / normal / excited / peak) and train a small classifier on audio and text. Expected to beat today's rule-based 55 % clearly; to be measured.
  • Speaker detection: enrol each commentator's voice before the match (built-in "enrollment file") rather than training.
  • Names in transcripts: a team/player name list (glossary) and a small text-correction step, rather than training.
Can you build the rest, or do we need a developer?

I can build all the code below in this workspace, including accounts, multiple sessions, separation, better speaker detection and a store-ready extension. What needs a person: paying for and creating accounts (Groq paid tier, GPU hosting, Chrome Web Store $5 developer fee, domain), legal and rights decisions (voice consent, broadcast rights), and testing on real matches you are allowed to use. A developer becomes useful once real clients rely on it (on-call fixes, monitoring, scaling across many servers).

00:07b · Publishing the extension

So anyone can install it

chrome://extensions only manages installed extensions; people find new ones in the Chrome Web Store (works in Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera, Arc). Publishing needs your Google account and a one-time $5 fee, so the upload is yours; everything else is ready in live-character-voice/extension-store/.

Open chrome.google.com/webstore/devconsole, sign in, pay the $5 registration.
New item → upload live-character-voice-extension.zip.
Paste the name, summary, description and permission reasons from LISTING.md; upload the 4 screenshots and the promo tile; privacy policy URL: https://manishvk18-live-character-voice.hf.space/privacy.html.
Choose Unlisted (only people with the link) or Public, give the reviewer an access code, submit. After approval (usually a few days) anyone clicks Add to Chrome. Edge Add-ons is free and takes the same zip.
00:08 · Test it yourself

How to check the numbers live

Open the hosted link → Quality Lab → pick a scored clip → Run accuracy test. Click Watch and listen to it live to see and hear the run while it is measured.
After about 4 minutes the result appears: felt delay, lines on time, lines voiced, transcription accuracy, what the voices said, speaker and emotion accuracy, which transcriber was used, missing words. Every past test and every session (including live streams) is listed with a link to its full report.
For live streams: start the extension, open its side panel; each line shows when it was voiced and how late. The session then appears in the Quality Lab list with its delay and line counts.
